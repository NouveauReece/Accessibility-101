# What is Accessibility

## Models of Disability
Quoted and modified from: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Models_of_disability

### Tragedy/Charity Models
* **View:** Disability is an individual "misfortune," and people with a disability are "victims"
* **Goal:** For able-bodied people to pity and provide charity to people with a disability, without regard for whether this charity was wanted or effective
* **Who has power:** Able-bodied people decide what is "charitable" and to what extent to provide that charity/pity

### Medical Model
* **View:** Disability as a medical disorder, in need of treatment and ultimately cure
* **Goal:** world where disability no longer exists, as all disabilities have been "cured"
* **Who has power:** physicians are the primary authorities on disability

### Social Model
* **View:** Impairment is the physical limits of one's bodymind, whereas disability is caused by society (e.g. a Deaf person wouldn't be "disabled" if most people used ASL and all videos had captions.)
* **Goal:** Embrace universal design such that society is altered to permit people with a disability to enjoy unrestricted social participation
* **Who has power:** Disabled activists, designers, and people with disabilities invited to the design process

### Critical Disability Studies Model (and other Poststructural models)
* **View:** Social forces shape and reinforce the category of "disability"
* **Goal:** Use a post-structural sociocultural lens to examine how the boundaries of disability shift across cultures and times. Its end goal is to decode and deconstruct the unstated assumptions about disability.
* **Who has power:** Disabled activists, designers, and those engaging with the post-structural sociocultural lens
